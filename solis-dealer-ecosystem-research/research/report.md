The project should have a stop condition. If existing portals already address the dealer problem at acceptable cost, concentrate on adoption, data quality and integration. If catalogue or transaction rights cannot be obtained, limit the pilot to controlled requests and deep links. If dealer task observation finds little avoidable work, revise the investment case.

## 3 Scope and research methodology

The research covers the global Solis brand, its actual European country routes, the Netherlands importer and dealer journey, and selected manufacturer/dealer benchmarks. Public evidence was accessed on 3 November 2026. Source publication periods are retained separately. The similar domain solis.eu was tested and excluded because it redirects to an appliance business. European tractor research uses Solis World's EU range, its official country selector and the relevant country/importer sites. [I01,I06]

The work combined source discovery with opening original pages, following public links and inspecting forms, catalogue descriptions, model options and financial disclosures. The Netherlands selector was followed through successive requirement choices, including a narrow-opening branch. An actual two-model comparison, the demonstration route, warranty form, model options, price list, importer listing, service and dealer pages were inspected. The Dutch site was rechecked specifically for this edition; access date does not establish when a feature was launched. No enquiry, application, order, warranty claim or payment was submitted. No private credentials were used. A public login proves an entry point, while public product documentation proves a described capability within its stated market.

**Evidence labels.** Verified means a reviewed reliable source directly supports the statement. Company claims remain identified as company descriptions. Reported means a third party or limited-access source states the claim. Inference is an analytical conclusion from cited evidence. Hypothesis is an untested operational proposition. Open question identifies information requiring ITL, importer, dealer or registry confirmation.

**Capability labels.** Verified present identifies a directly observed public capability or documented entry point. Partially verified means part of the journey was inspected but execution or coverage was not. Not publicly discoverable records that research did not locate the capability, without concluding it is absent. Unclear / requires direct confirmation is used where scope, permissions or system behaviour cannot be established.

Evidence quality prioritises audited filings, official corporate and product documentation, registry information and credible industry research. Forums are qualitative signals with unverified identities. Search snippets were used for discovery; inaccessible originals are recorded as limitations rather than silently accepted. No paid survey results were obtained.

An initial workspace inventory found no earlier Solis research document among the available document files. The research therefore proceeded independently. Material corrections to common assumptions are explicit: EU namesake excluded; existing selector/configurator/commerce recognised; private portal clues retained; Dutch and global parts routes separated; peer economics never presented as Solis economics.

The complete register provides source titles, publishers, direct URLs, access dates, supported claims and limitations. Cross-references such as [I08] point to that register and are linked in the editable report. Source duplicates are retained where separate research areas used the same original, and can be matched by URL.

## 4 Solis and ITL business overview

Solis is the tractor brand presented by International Tractors Limited. The global website distinguishes EU and global ranges and links visitors to local distributors. ITL's own history and generic heritage wording differ across pages; the report does not turn marketing heritage dates or global scale claims into audited brand figures. Local model, engine, emissions and warranty variants matter for digital product data. [I02,I06,I13]

The likely commercial chain is manufacturer to regional/importer organisation to authorised dealer to owner. This is a useful analytical map, but the legal seller, invoice issuer, stock owner, warranty obligor and data controller can differ by transaction. An importer that also has retail listings may act in more than one role. Each proposed workflow must retain these distinctions.

Public Netherlands journeys combine brand discovery, dealer advice and an importer ecommerce route. A prospective owner can identify candidates and request a quote; a local dealer remains important for configuration, installation, demonstration, handover and service. Parts support depends on the exact machine and component identity, not simply its public model name. [I08,I09,I20]

**Financial scale is available at ITL level only.** ICRA reproduces FY2025 consolidated operating income of INR10,021.8 crore and profit after tax of INR1,780.9 crore, compared with INR9,485.4 crore and INR1,740.3 crore in FY2024. These are reported ITL indicators, not Solis-brand or Netherlands turnover. The report did not obtain ITL's full audited statements or a Solis revenue split. [F01]

**Implication.** A local product needs a bounded business case. Neither ITL scale nor the global dealer network establishes the Dutch volume available to a digital service. The necessary denominators are active Dutch dealers, sold and supported machines, eligible leads, parts requests and invoice lines over a defined period.

## 7 Dealer ecosystem and customer journeys

The customer journey begins with a task: mowing, material movement, maintenance or another defined use. Public discovery should retain that context through model selection, configuration and dealer discussion. The dealer then validates fit, conditions, delivery and support. After delivery, the machine identity should connect the owner to correct documentation, maintenance and parts.

**Proposed connected journey.** Capture requirements with a versioned configuration; create a consented enquiry; assign an eligible dealer; acknowledge ownership; prepare a human-approved quote; preserve order and delivery references; register machine identity where authorised; link future parts/service requests to that machine. Each state needs a responsible organisation and a visible exception route.

For a parts request, the commercial journey is different. A customer may know only a symptom, photograph or model. The dealer must establish machine and component identity, validate a candidate against build/serial applicability, confirm supply and decide how to handle ambiguity. The architecture should support a request with missing information rather than invent a definitive part.

For a new dealer, the customer is the applicant business. Submission begins qualification; it does not confer territory, credit, warranty authority or wholesale prices. Readiness requires completed approvals, agreements, training and tested access. An applicant status page can reduce chasing even if approvals remain human decisions.

![Dealer journey](Visual_Assets/03_dealer_journey.png)

### Proposed service responsibilities

ITL provides product authority and confirms which brand systems can be reused. The importer owns the local coordination decision after its legal mandate is verified. Dealers own customer follow-up and service execution within their contracts. A central support team handles unassigned leads, identification ambiguity, unavailable stock feeds and failed system handoffs. Responsibility must remain explicit when a workflow spans organisations.

### Roles and permissions

An applicant sees its own evidence and status. A dealer principal manages its staff and commercial approvals within assigned scope. Sales staff see permitted leads, configurations and quotes. Parts staff see licensed catalogue and authorised availability/prices. Technicians see machine/support data relevant to their work. Importer operations manage exceptions and distribution; network development manages qualification; finance/commercial owners approve credit and price entitlements; ITL product/support owners govern technical authority. Administrators manage identity without automatic rights to every commercial record.

Permissions should combine organisation, market, territory, role, contract status and record relationship. A suspended dealer must lose transaction rights promptly. A technician should not inherit dealership-wide margin visibility. Delegated access, staff departure and dealership transfer need explicit workflows.

### Major workflow

A configured enquiry enters the authorised queue with consent and source revision. The dealer accepts ownership or returns it with a reason. A quote draft references the approved configuration and price version. An authorised person reviews terms and sends it. Any resulting order retains the quote reference; delivery adds a verified machine serial where available. Failed integration leaves a visible retry/exception state rather than an apparent successful order. This is a proposed workflow requiring system demonstrations.

## 10 Parts Intelligence research and proposed capabilities

The public Dutch support route is dealer assisted. Solis's global parts page describes a German/Yanmar distribution arrangement, while Dutch information describes an operational route via Almere. These may reflect different network layers, markets or dates. Do not assume a single fulfilment centre or treat a marketing delivery promise as an available-to-promise guarantee. Confirm who holds each stock item and which system acknowledges the order. [I20,I21,I22]

MyCNHStore documents serial/model discovery, assembly diagrams and fitment. Fendt's parts resources describe exploded views and a catalogue-to-request basket. Kubota Europe separates machine parts from industrial engine parts. These patterns support a machine-first identification experience and show why a catalogue and a commerce system should have a controlled handoff. [B02,B08,B01]

### Proposed identification journey

1. Establish full tractor serial, model, market and build date; collect a nameplate photograph when useful.
2. Resolve configuration and fitted assembly; collect engine/component model and serial if relevant.
3. Search licensed text and diagram data, preserving source revision and assembly context.
4. Verify serial applicability, quantity, supersession and approved substitute rules. Escalate uncertain cases.
5. Retrieve authorised price, stock timestamp, reservation status and lead time. Distinguish a stock snapshot from confirmed supply.
6. Obtain dealer approval, send the request/order and wait for authoritative acknowledgement.
7. Record shipment and installation/work-order references where permitted; capture returns and incorrect-part reasons for improvement.

![Parts identification journey](Visual_Assets/09_parts_journey.png)

### Third party engines and catalogue rights

The global S26 page identifies a Mitsubishi engine, and Netherlands material distinguishes component/engine sources across the range. This is model-specific evidence. A Yanmar commercial relationship does not prove that every Solis tractor has a Yanmar engine. DET's Mitsubishi-distributor FAQ requests engine model, serial and application, and describes purchased manuals and restricted ordering accounts. It does not establish DET as the supplier for every Solis machine. [B14,B15,I21]

Permission to view a catalogue is separate from rights to redistribute diagrams, build a search index, use images for matching, embed data in an assistant or train a model. Obtain written coverage and permitted-use terms from ITL and any component/catalogue rights holder. Catalogue access should not be bypassed by scraping.

### Proposed data and integration model

![Parts ecosystem map](Visual_Assets/17_parts_ecosystem.png)

| Record | Minimum fields or relationships | Proposed authority to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Machine and build | Serial, model code, market, build date, configuration, component identities | ITL engineering/build records |
| Catalogue and diagram | Assembly, revision, hotspot, document/source key, licence scope | ITL or licensed catalogue owner |
| Part and fitment | Exact identifier, descriptions, applicability ranges, quantity, supersession | Approved part master and engineering rules |
| Implement compatibility | Mounting, PTO, hydraulic, load and market conditions | Approved manufacturer/importer rules |
| Supply and entitlement | Warehouse, timestamp, available-to-promise definition, authorised price/tax | Importer ERP and warehouse systems, unverified |
| Order and outcome | Acknowledgement, shipment, work order, return reason, claim reference | Seller, fulfilment and dealer records, unverified |

Every imported record needs its source key, owner, version, last update and permitted use. Compatibility is a structured rule requiring evidence; it must not be inferred from similar names or photographs. Genuine/aftermarket origin should be explicit, and any approved substitution or warranty implication must come from the applicable commercial policy.

**Proposed first release.** An authorised parts desk with machine identity, source-linked candidates, fitment status, stock freshness and expert review. Text/OCR and image search can suggest candidates; they cannot establish fitment alone. Add customer self-service and ordering only when catalogue coverage, prices, returns, availability and support responsibilities are proven.

### Deployment evidence and its limits

AGCO describes AgBot integrated with AGCONet for dealer parts/service questions. This is a manufacturer-described capability, not independently measured performance. Caterpillar's March 2026 release describes the off-board assistant as available and the in-cab version as a prototype. Its support page distinguishes guest information and personalised account context. These sources do not prove unrestricted autonomous purchasing. [M02,M03,M04]

Deere's September 2026 JD announcement was discoverable but the original opened as a JavaScript shell. It is retained as limited-access evidence of announced early access, not proof of a general rollout. A named Ajusa implementation case reports SAP-connected order creation/modification with confirmations and audit logs; this is implementer-reported production, without customer-side confirmation or verified outcome metrics. Neither example supplies a Solis ROI assumption. [M05,M06]

### Proposed Solis concept

Start by collecting work requirements, dimensions, terrain, desired implements and the market. Retrieve current authorised model and option facts. Explain a shortlist with page/specification references and unresolved requirements. Preserve the conversation and requirements in a structured dealer handoff, subject to the user's consent and the dealer's permissions.

With a verified catalogue, assist parts-counter staff in finding candidates and missing identifiers. With authorised price feeds, prepare quote drafts that retain source price revision, tax treatment, configuration, validity and approver. Dealer/stock-aware routing requires territory, capacity and availability data; a public dealer list alone cannot support a stock promise.

**Now, subject to content rights.** A controlled prototype can answer from a small curated public/document set, collect requirements and prepare a staff-reviewed handoff. **After integration.** Read authenticated machine/order data, check prices and availability, stage quote/order drafts. **After separate validation.** Permit narrowly bounded writes with approval, acknowledgement, retries and rollback/compensation rules. Autonomous discounts, credit changes, warranty awards, unverified substitutions and binding orders should not be in the initial scope.

### Guardrails and release evidence

Use an authoritative source allowlist with document revisions. Filter retrieval by organisation, market and role before model access. Treat retrieved text, uploads and customer messages as data, never as instructions to override permissions. Tools must independently enforce identity, entitlements and allowed actions. Separate read, draft and execute permissions.

Require authoritative fitment evidence before presenting a part as compatible. If machine identity or serial range is unresolved, ask for information or escalate. An internal model confidence score is insufficient. For a draft quote, recheck pricing and availability at approval time and record the exact content approved. Use idempotent transaction keys, authoritative acknowledgements, complete audit trails and visible failure states.

Test grounded answer accuracy, wrong-model contamination, unsupported pricing, prompt injection, cross-dealer access, stale data, failed acknowledgements and approval bypass. Retain only necessary conversation data under agreed access and retention rules. Staff must be able to correct sources, stop the assistant and take over the case. Targets should be agreed from a reviewed test set, with all consequential errors investigated before wider release.

## 22 Conclusions and recommendations

The research supports a connected ecosystem opportunity, with reuse and evidence gathering as the immediate actions. Public Netherlands discovery and commerce are sufficiently developed that another selector or storefront needs a specific, demonstrated rationale. Regional portal evidence makes an ITL/importer walkthrough essential before procurement. [I07,I08,I09,I10,I11,M10]

**First, establish authority.** Resolve the legal sponsor, dealer contracts and data rights. ITL's reported ownership of Solis B.V. and AgriXpert's self-described importer role do not determine which entity can approve every workflow. [F01,I04,I05]

**Second, observe and measure.** Validate dealer tasks, private systems and catalogue coverage using real records. Treat rekeying, chasing and identification difficulty as hypotheses until observed. Establish entity-specific financial baselines; peer parts economics justify investigation without supplying a Solis percentage.

**Third, connect a bounded pilot.** Preserve enquiry context, give dealers a clear queue, improve verified parts identification and track onboarding readiness. Use existing transaction systems and catalogue tools where suitable. Keep exceptions visible and decisions with authorised owners.

**Fourth, add AI where it earns its place.** Source-linked answers and structured handoffs can be tested before transactional agency. Quote/order actions require approved prices, verified fitment, identity controls, explicit human approval and authoritative acknowledgement. No peer assistant announcement establishes Solis readiness.

**Finally, decide with measured evidence.** Broaden only when the pilot improves comparable tasks, resolves material failures and has an accountable operating model. If existing tools already meet the need, invest in configuration, data quality and adoption. If the data cannot support safe parts or commercial automation, retain human-assisted requests and narrow the scope.
