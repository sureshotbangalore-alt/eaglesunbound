# Financial evidence and business case

Research cut off 3 November 2026. Currency values retain their original units. Financial years are entity specific. No Solis percentage is inferred from peers.

## What the public evidence supports

**Reported — ITL financial scale.** ICRA's 28 November 2025 rationale reproduces audited indicators in INR crore. Standalone operating income rose from 9,352.5 in FY2024 to 9,789.7 in FY2025; PAT from 1,757.2 to 1,818.8. Consolidated operating income rose from 9,485.4 to 10,021.8, and PAT from 1,740.3 to 1,780.9. Consolidated growth was 10,021.8 / 9,485.4 − 1 = 5.66%. These are ITL figures, not Solis or Dutch revenue. No machine/parts split is disclosed. ICRA reports FY2025 exports of approximately 30,000 tractors, approximately 20% of volume, with overseas volumes falling. Annexure II lists Solis Tractors & Agricultural Machinery B.V. as 100% ITL owned. The rating methodology says finance is excluded while the consolidation annexure includes Autotrac Finance: request the underlying accounts to reconcile scope. FY2026 audited statements were not obtained. [F01 ICRA pp1–4 and 6](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=139360).

**Verified — AGCO product mix.** Its 2025 annual report explicitly separates tractors, replacement parts, grain systems and other machinery. Replacement parts / consolidated net sales: 2023, 1,816.0 / 14,412.4 = 12.60%; 2024, 1,827.8 / 11,661.9 = 15.67%; 2025, 1,873.3 / 10,082.0 = 18.58% (USD millions). Tractor revenue was 8,711.0, 7,059.3 and 6,693.0 respectively. The residual is not all machines: grain systems and other equipment must remain separate. Rising parts share partly reflects a falling denominator; it does not establish digital causation. The November 2024 G&P divestiture also changes group mix. Europe/Middle East replacement parts were 1,227.6 / regional sales 6,736.7 = 18.22% in 2025, not a Dutch figure. Replacement parts exclude any separately unidentified services. [F02 AGCO annual report pp113–115](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/880266/000088026626000027/agco2025annualreport_ars.htm).

**Verified — dealer economics.** Titan Machinery's year ended 31 January 2026 provides a dealer-group comparison, covering agriculture, construction, Europe and Australia. USD millions: equipment 1,774.535; parts 428.261; service 177.910; rental/other 46.401; total 2,427.107. Parts share = 17.64%; service = 7.33%; combined = 24.98%. Gross profit: equipment 129.330, parts 132.515, service 109.459, rental/other 11.252, total 382.556. Parts plus service contribute 63.25% of gross profit. Category gross margins are 7.3%, 30.9% and 61.5% respectively. Gross profit is before operating expenses; service cost classification and retail versus OEM accounting prevent direct application to Solis. Agriculture-segment revenue of 1,557.814 includes parts 286.927 (18.42%) and service 127.186 (8.16%). [F03 Titan 10-K pp31–33 and Note 3 p62](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1409171/000162828026022376/titn-20260131.htm).

## Interpretation and decision

**Inference.** The evidence justifies measuring parts retention, identification effort and service administration alongside machine lead conversion. It does not justify a Solis revenue uplift, budget or ROI. Authorise data discovery and a measured pilot; allocate development investment only after obtaining local baselines, contracts and technical access.

No public Solis-specific parts turnover, gross margin, Netherlands installed base, retained parts spend, warranty cost per machine, dealer labour rate, lead conversion, or portal adoption baseline was established. Request 24 months of invoice-line data separated into machines, genuine parts, implements, service labour and warranty reimbursements; dealer and machine identifiers; gross profit definitions; returns reasons; quote history; stock fill rate; channel/territory; and catalogue licensing. Reconcile ledger totals before building retention measures.

## Formula framework

Dealer productivity capacity = eligible annual requests × adopted share × minutes saved / 60. Monetary capacity equivalent = hours × verified loaded hourly cost. Cash savings require an explicit staffing/overtime or outsourcing decision; do not add the full capacity value to cash savings.

Wrong-part costs avoided = eligible part orders × adopted share × reduction in wrong-part return rate (percentage points) × avoidable cost per return. Include freight, restocking and handling only once. The labour rate calculation must exclude return handling if that labour is already captured here.

Incremental retained parts gross profit = eligible lost demand × attributable capture improvement × net basket value × incremental contribution margin, less added fulfilment/returns/support cost. Measure transaction lines and use a baseline/control. Do not apply a manufacturer share to retail invoices.

Incremental machine contribution = qualified leads × attributable conversion change × contribution per incremental sale, less variable acquisition and fulfilment cost. Quote speed alone is not a sales effect.

Onboarding capacity = annual applicants × adoption × hours saved per applicant × loaded cost; independently track elapsed approval time and first-order readiness. Exclude work merely shifted to another role.

Net annual benefit = attributable cash cost avoided + incremental contribution − annual run and change costs. Investment payback and ROI remain unavailable until approved implementation cost, timing and risk assumptions are supplied. Do not invent them.

## Illustrative model only

The workbook contains one selectable low/base/high build with the same 1,000 eligible requests used as a normalised example. These are design assumptions, not observed Solis volumes or forecasts. Low/base/high inputs: minutes saved 5/10/15; adoption 30%/60%/80%; illustrative loaded cost EUR35/hour; wrong-part return-rate reduction 1/2/3 percentage points (from an illustrative 8%); avoidable non-labour cost EUR40/return. Capacity hours = 25/100/200; capacity equivalents EUR875/3,500/7,000. Avoided returns = 3/12/24; non-labour value EUR120/480/960. Combined gross opportunity equivalent = EUR995/3,980/7,960 per 1,000 eligible annual requests, before implementation/run cost and without claiming cash savings. No Solis ROI is calculated.

The illustration assumes one eligible parts order for every identification request. Actual modelling must separate request volume from fulfilled-order volume whenever that relationship differs. The 6% base return-rate result applies to adopted requests; the blended eligible-order return rate would be 6.8% at 60% adoption.

Sensitivity: each additional minute saved per 1,000 requests at 60% adoption adds 10 capacity hours, equivalent to EUR350 at the illustrative hourly cost. Each additional percentage point reduction at 60% adoption avoids six returns, equivalent to EUR240 at illustrative EUR40 non-labour cost. These are derivatives of the illustrative formula, not external findings.

The actual Solis input area is intentionally blank. Missing prerequisites propagate as n.a.; zero inputs remain legitimate zeros. Business owners must replace volume, adoption, savings, cost, return rate, contribution and operating-cost assumptions with measured values.
